# Straight Lines

Everyone can draw a straight line — slowly, with a ruler. Drawing one *quickly and freehand* is a skill, and it is the first one worth having, because confident lines make everything else look intentional.

:::important title="The one rule"
Move your **whole arm**, not your fingers. Lock the wrist, ghost the stroke in the air two or three times, then commit in one motion.
:::

## Ghost, then commit

:::widget type="multiple-choice" id="lines-why-ghost"
prompt: Why ghost the stroke in the air before touching the page?
options:
  - text: To warm up the muscles
    explanation: 'A general warm-up helps, but ghosting rehearses the *particular* line you are about to draw — its start, end and direction — not just loosens the arm.'
  - text: To rehearse the exact motion so the real stroke is a repetition, not an attempt
    correct: true
  - text: To check the pen works
    explanation: 'You could check a pen with one scribble. Ghosting is repeated on every stroke, and it is about the motion, not the tool.'
hint: 'Think about what the hand has done just before the pen touches down.'
explanation: 'A confident line comes from a motion you have already made. Ghosting rehearses the exact path, speed and direction two or three times, so by the time the pen lands, your arm is repeating something it knows instead of guessing. That is why ghosted lines come out straighter and faster than careful, slow ones.'
:::

## Exercise 1 — trace, then free

Trace the grey lines first. Then turn tracing off and draw the same lines from memory, between the same points.

:::widget type="sketch" id="lines-trace"
prompt: |
  Trace the five horizontal lines **in one stroke each**, left to right. Then the five diagonals, top to bottom.

  Turn the trace off and repeat beside them.
trace: assets/images/trace-lines.png
traceOpacity: 0.25
widths: [3, 5]
minStrokes: 10
rubric:
  - Each line is a single stroke with no retouching
  - The line is straight in the middle, even if the ends wobble
  - I drew the free lines at least as fast as the traced ones
explanation: 'Tracing takes the question of *where* off the table, so all your attention goes to *how* — one stroke, from the shoulder, at a steady speed. A line drawn quickly is straighter than a slow one, because a slow line follows every tremor in your hand. The wobble at the ends matters less than a straight middle.'
:::

:::tip title="Wobble is fine"
A fast line that wobbles looks better than a slow one that crawls. Speed first; accuracy comes with repetition.
:::

## Exercise 2 — connect the dots

:::widget type="sketch" id="lines-free"
prompt: |
  No trace this time. Fill the canvas with straight lines in every direction. Aim for **twenty** strokes. Try to make a few lines pass *exactly* through the end of an earlier one.
widths: [3]
minStrokes: 20
rubric:
  - I made at least twenty lines
  - I drew from the shoulder for every one
explanation: 'Hitting the end of an earlier line is the real skill. Drawing is mostly connecting points you have placed — the corner of a box, the edge of a rim. A line that is straight but lands in the wrong place is still a wrong line, so practise accuracy and confidence together.'
:::
