# The Mug

Look at the mug before you draw anything. Not at *a mug* — at **this** one.

![A red mug on a grey table, seen from slightly above](assets/images/ref-mug.png)

## See it as shapes

:::widget type="multiple-choice" id="mug-shapes"
prompt: Which simple shapes make up this mug?
multiple: true
options:
  - text: A cylinder — a rectangle with an ellipse top and bottom
    correct: true
  - text: A torus — the handle is a ring seen edge-on
    correct: true
  - text: A sphere
    explanation: 'A sphere has no straight sides. The mug''s body has straight vertical sides — look at its profile.'
  - text: A cone
    explanation: 'A cone narrows to one end. This mug is about as wide at the base as at the rim.'
hint: 'Look at the body and the handle separately. Which simple solid does each one resemble?'
explanation: 'The body is a cylinder — a rectangle in profile with an ellipse at the rim and another at the base. The handle is part of a torus, a ring, seen from the side. Seeing objects as simple solids first is what keeps the proportions right before any detail goes in.'
:::

## Exercise 4 — build it

Draw the mug in three passes: the construction (light, fast), the contour (one confident line), the handle. Trace first if you like, then do it beside the trace.

:::widget type="sketch" id="mug-draw"
prompt: |
  1. **Construction** — a light vertical centre line, a rectangle for the body, an ellipse for the rim.
  2. **Contour** — one clean line over the construction.
  3. **Handle** — two curves, thicker at the top where it joins the body.

  Press *Done* when you have all three. Then compare with the instructor's drawing.
reference: assets/images/ref-mug.png
trace: assets/images/trace-mug.png
traceOpacity: 0.2
model: assets/images/model-mug.png
palette: ["#1b2030", "#8a90a0", "#c2410c"]
widths: [2, 4, 8]
minStrokes: 4
rubric:
  - The rim ellipse is wider than it is tall, not a circle
  - The bottom of the mug curves the same way as the rim
  - The handle attaches at the side, not at the edge of the rim
  - My construction lines are lighter than my contour
explanation: 'The order is the lesson. Construction lines get the proportions right while they are still cheap to change, and the contour then commits to them. The base ellipse must curve the same way as the rim, because both are circles seen from the same angle. Most mugs that look wrong have a flat base under a curved rim.'
:::

:::note title="About the comparison"
The instructor's version is not the *right* answer. Look for differences in **proportion** — is your rim too round? your handle too small? — not in line quality.
:::

rubric:
  - I started with the big shape, not the stem
  - The apple is slightly wider than it is tall
  - The highlight sits on the upper left, where the light comes from
explanation: 'Starting with the big shape means the stem and leaf have something correct to sit on. If you start with the details, the overall shape grows around them, usually too small or lopsided. The three-minute limit is deliberate — it keeps you working on the whole drawing, not polishing one corner.'
