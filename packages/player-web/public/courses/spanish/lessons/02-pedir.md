:::tip title="Un or una?"
Every noun has a gender. *Un café*, *un té*, *un zumo* — but *una tostada*, *una cerveza*. There is no rule that always works; learn the article with the word.
:::

## Listen to the conversation

A customer walks into a café in the morning. Listen **twice at most**, then answer the three questions. The transcript unlocks once you have answered all three.

::::listening src="assets/audio/dialogo-1.mp3" playLimit=2 speed="0.75,1"
:::transcript
— Buenos días, ¿qué desea?
— Hola. Un café con leche y una tostada, por favor.
— ¿Algo más?
— No, gracias. ¿Cuánto es?
— Son tres euros con cincuenta.
:::

  - text: A coffee with milk
    correct: true

explanation: 'The customer says *un café con leche*, a coffee with milk. In Spain a plain *café* is a small, strong espresso, so if you want milk you have to say so.'
:::

:::widget type="multiple-choice" id="pedir-dialogo-1-q2"
prompt: What else does the customer order?
options:
  - text: Nothing else
    explanation: 'The customer does say *no, gracias* — but only when the waiter asks if they want anything *more*. By then they have already ordered two things. Listen for the word after *y*.'
  - text: A croissant
    explanation: 'A croissant would be *un cruasán*, and it does not come up here. Listen for the word after *y*.'
  - text: Toast
    correct: true
hint: 'It comes straight after the drink, joined with *y* (and).'
explanation: '*Un café con leche **y una tostada***. *Una tostada* is toast, usually with olive oil and tomato — the classic Spanish breakfast. The *una* tells you *tostada* is feminine.'
:::

:::widget type="fill-blank" id="pedir-dialogo-1-q3"
text: "The waiter asks *¿Algo {{más|mas}}?* — anything else? The customer answers *No, {{gracias}}*."
hint: 'The first word is the one for *more*. The second is the word you have used most so far.'
explanation: '*¿Algo más?* is literally *something more?* — the waiter''s standard question. *No, gracias* is the polite way to close the order. *Más* has an accent to tell it apart from *mas*, an old literary word for *but*. This exercise accepts it without the accent too.'
:::
::::

:::details summary="Useful phrases from the dialogue"
- **¿Qué desea?** — What would you like? (formal)
- **¿Algo más?** — Anything else?
- **¿Cuánto es?** — How much is it?
:::

## Now you

Write the customer's full order, exactly as you hear it.

:::widget type="dictation" id="pedir-dictado"
audio:
  src: assets/audio/dictado-1.mp3
  transcript: Un café con leche, por favor.
  playLimit: 4
  speed: [0.75, 1]
answer: Un café con leche, por favor
alternatives:
  - Un cafe con leche por favor
hint: 'Six words. It starts with the article.'
explanation: '*Un café con leche, por favor* — article, drink, then the polite ending. The accent on *café* marks the stress on the last syllable, ca-FÉ. A comma before *por favor* is normal, but the check ignores punctuation, because you cannot hear it.'
:::

Then say it yourself. Listen to the model first, record, and compare.
